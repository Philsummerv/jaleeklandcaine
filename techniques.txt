# How to get around, and how to handle mobs and blocks. The bot's practical know-how.
#
# The manifesto says WHO the bot is - what it wants, what it refuses to do, how it carries
# itself. This file is the technique underneath that: how to actually climb, dig, fight and
# get unstuck. Where the two conflict, the manifesto wins ("never run from a fight" beats
# "back away from a creeper").
#
# Lines starting with # are ignored. The running bot re-reads this file within a couple of
# seconds of a save, and unlike the manifesto it costs NOTHING to change - it is handed to
# Claude as written, not compiled. Add whatever you learn from watching it play.

## Reading the screen before you decide
- The crosshair is the exact centre. You affect whatever is under it, nothing else.
- A black outline is drawn around the block under the crosshair. No outline, no block: you
  are aimed at air, or past your four-block reach.
- The hotbar is the row of nine slots at the bottom. A number in the corner of a slot is
  how many of that item you have. An empty hotbar means you are holding nothing and have
  collected nothing yet.
- Items you have picked up appear in the hotbar within a second. If you broke something and
  no slot changed, you did not collect it - walk over where it fell.
- Hearts, top left of the HUD, are health. Drumsticks are hunger. Both are ten icons.
- A black panel over the middle of the screen is a menu, not the world.
- Distance is hard to judge from a screenshot. If something looks two blocks away it is
  often five. When in doubt, walk toward it and look again rather than acting at range.

## Getting around
- To climb a one-block step, walk forward with jump held. Jumping on the spot just lands
  you where you started.
- One block is the limit. If a wall is two blocks tall, stop trying: walk along it and look
  for a lower step, or mine through it.
- To get out of a pit, mine the wall in front of you at head height and then at your feet,
  and walk out through the gap. Or look straight down, jump, and place a block under
  yourself as you rise - repeat that to climb anywhere.
- Near a drop, move with sneak on. It stops you walking off the edge.
- Pick your line before you set off: aim for the slope or the terrace that steps down
  gently, rather than walking at a cliff and hoping.
- Drops over three blocks hurt, and badly over five. Go around, or step down in stages.
- Water is a safe way down and you can swim back up it.
- Never dig straight down - you cannot see what you are standing over.

## Searching for something you cannot see yet
- Pick a bearing and hold it. Walking twenty seconds in one direction gets you somewhere;
  re-deciding every three seconds leaves you where you started.
- Look level, or slightly up, while travelling. Trees, hills and mobs are all at or above
  eye level, and nothing you need to find is at your feet.
- Climb for a view. The top of a ridge shows you where the green is; from the bottom of a
  canyon you can see nothing but walls.
- Before setting off, turn a full circle in three or four steps and note what is in each
  direction, then commit to the best one.
- If the journal shows you have been in the same place for a minute, the approach is not
  working. Leave, do not keep adjusting.

## Aiming at something you can see
- The view is roughly 70 degrees across and 40 high. So to turn to something: how far it
  sits from the centre, as a fraction of the half-width, times 35 degrees. A quarter of the
  way to the left edge is about yaw -9; halfway, about -18; at the very edge, about -35.
  Use the same with 20 degrees for pitch, positive up.
- Make the turn, then look at the next screenshot before swinging. One turn is rarely exact,
  and a second small correction is cheaper than hitting the ground.
- Standing right next to something means looking slightly DOWN at it, not level - mobs and
  blocks at your feet are below the centre line.

## Breaking and placing blocks
- Hold the button down to break. Separate clicks restart the break each time and never
  finish it. Use mine for blocks, never attack.
- Check the aim first: no outline means no block. If you can see there is no outline, do
  not mine anyway - move, then look again. Mining at nothing wastes the whole hold.
- Expect roughly hardness x 5 seconds bare-handed: leaves instant, dirt about 2s, wood
  about 3s, stone and terracotta about 7s. Give a stony block 12 seconds or the hold ends
  just before it gives and the progress resets.
- Match the tool: axe for wood, pickaxe for stone, shovel for dirt and sand.
- Stone, terracotta and ore drop nothing without a pickaxe in hand - bare-handed you are
  only clearing a path. Wood, dirt, sand and leaves always drop.
- Walk over what you break to pick it up. Drops sit where the block was for a few minutes.
- To place a block, aim at the face of a block next to where you want it and use.

## Getting wood
- Logs are the trunk, not the leaves. Leaves break instantly and give almost nothing.
- Leaves get in the way. On a spruce or any dense tree the trunk is usually behind a screen
  of leaves, and you cannot reach through them: break the leaves in front first, then the
  trunk behind. They take no time at all.
- A trunk seen from a few blocks away is often not the block under your crosshair. The
  thing you are aimed at may be the ground or a leaf in front of it, which looks nearly the
  same from a distance. Walk right up to the trunk until it fills the middle of the screen
  before you start.
- Work a trunk from the bottom up. Break the lowest log, step into the gap, and the next is
  at head height. The highest ones may need a jump or a block to stand on.
- Spruce trunks are dark brown with grey-ish bark and grow tall and straight, often in a
  2x2 block of four trunks - each one is a separate column to break.
- Oak and birch are short and bushy; acacia leans diagonally, so its trunk may not be
  directly under its leaves.
- One tree is four to six logs, sometimes more. Finish the tree you are on before looking
  for another.
- If the ground is orange terracotta with no grass, there is no wood here worth hunting.
  Head for green hills or a forest you can see in the distance.
- Replant a sapling on grass when you have one, so there is wood here next time.

## Making tools
- The order is logs, planks, sticks, crafting table, wooden pickaxe, cobblestone, stone
  tools. No rung can be skipped.
- One log gives four planks; two planks give sticks; four planks give a crafting table.
- A pickaxe, axe or sword needs a table placed in the world - the 2x2 grid in your own
  inventory is too small.
- To use a table: select it in the hotbar, aim at flat ground, use to place it, then look
  at it and use again to open it.
- In Bedrock the recipes you can currently make are listed down the side of the crafting
  screen. Click the recipe, then the result; no arranging in a grid.
- Select a tool in the hotbar before using it. Carrying an axe does nothing if you are
  still holding nothing.

## Dealing with mobs
- Swing while backing away rather than standing still, and re-aim between hits - they move.
- Do not swing before the mob is under the crosshair. Hitting the terrain next to it does
  nothing, and the swing cooldown means a wasted swing costs you the next real one.
- Zombies and spiders have to reach you, so retreating while you hit them works well.
- Skeletons shoot from range. Close the distance fast or put a block between you; standing
  still in the open is the worst option.
- Creepers hiss before they explode. Hit, step back, hit, step back - never stand beside one.
- Swings have a cooldown. Timed hits do far more than spamming clicks.
- Taking damage with nothing in view means it is behind you. Turn around.
- They burn off at sunrise in the open, but not in caves or under cover.
- Health comes back on its own while hunger is nearly full, so eating is how you heal.

## Getting through the night
- Mobs appear in the dark. Torches both light the way and stop them spawning nearby.
- The fast shelter is a hole: dig two blocks into a hillside, step in, seal the entrance
  behind you, and wait for light.
- Charcoal comes from burning logs in a furnace; a torch is charcoal plus a stick.

## Menus and screens
- The death screen has a green Respawn button in the middle. Nothing works until it is
  clicked, so click it first.
- The pause menu's Resume Game button sits at the top of the menu panel.
- Escape closes a menu you opened. With no menu open it opens the pause menu and freezes
  the game, so only ever use it to close something.
- Movement keys go into an open menu instead of the world. Close it before moving.

## When something is not working
- "NOTHING HAPPENED" after a mine: you were not aimed at a block. Walking closer fixes this
  far more often than holding longer.
- Mining that cracks but never breaks: the block is too hard for your hands. Leave it and
  find wood, or come back with the right tool.
- Walking but not moving: a wall or a pit. Jump forward, or turn and go another way.
- Nothing in the hotbar after breaking things: you have not walked over the drops.
- The same view for several plans running: you are not actually travelling. Commit to one
  direction and hold it.
- Health falling with nothing in sight: turn around, then deal with what is there.
- Repeating a plan that did nothing the last three times is the one thing never worth
  doing. Change something - position, aim, direction, or target.
